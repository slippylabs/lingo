In this lesson: **good evening**, **please**, and **telling someone your name**.

## Greetings

- **Goedenavond** — Good evening
- **Goedenavond en bedankt.** — Good evening and thanks.

## Two words for "you"

Dutch has a friendly "you" for friends and family, and a polite "you" for strangers, shops and work:

- **je** (or **jij**) — you, friendly
- **u** — you, polite

"Please" changes to match:

- **alsjeblieft** — please (friendly)
- **alstublieft** — please (polite)

Examples:

- **Wat is je naam?** — What's your name? (friendly)
- **Uw naam, alstublieft?** — Your name, please? (polite)
- **Hoe gaat het met jou?** — How are you? (friendly)

## Saying who you are

- **Mijn naam is Cecille.** — My name is Cecille.
- **Ik kom uit Den Haag.** — I come from The Hague.

> **Watch out:** the letters **ij** (as in **mijn**) make one sound, a bit like the "ay" in "say". They are not an "i" followed by a "j".
