In this lesson: **sports**, the **accusative case** (the form for the thing a verb acts on) and the **locative case** (the form for where something is).

## What you play: accusative

After *grać w* (to play), the sport changes. Feminine words ending in **‑a** take **‑ę**:

- **Ona gra w koszykówkę.** — She plays basketball. (koszykówka → koszykówkę)
- **Gram w piłkę nożną.** — I play football.

Some masculine sport names add **‑a**:

- **On gra w tenisa.** — He plays tennis.
- **Ja nie gram w hokeja.** — I do not play hockey.

## Where: locative

After **w** (in) and **na** (at, on), when you say where something is, the ending changes again:

- **My pływamy w rzece.** — We swim in the river. (rzeka → rzece)
- **Stadion jest w mieście.** — The stadium is in the city. (miasto → mieście)
- **Ona gra na stadionie.** — She plays at the stadium.

## Useful phrases

- **On trenuje każdego dnia.** — He trains every day.
- **Oni lubią sport.** — They like sport.
- **Trzeba trenować.** — It is necessary to train.

> **Watch out:** **w** has two jobs here: **Gram w piłkę nożną** (playing a game) but **My pływamy w rzece** (in a place). The endings differ.
