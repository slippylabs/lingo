In this lesson: **family words** and the **instrumental case**, the form a word takes after **z** ("with").

## "With": z + instrumental

A case is a change of ending that shows a word's job in the sentence. After **z** (with), the noun takes the instrumental case:

- **Ona idzie z matką.** — She is going with her mother. (matka → matką)
- **Oni jadą z ciotką.** — They are going with their aunt.
- **On mówi z dziadkiem.** — He is talking with his grandfather. (dziadek → dziadkiem)
- **On żyje z synem.** — He lives with his son.

Feminine words ending in **‑a** take **‑ą**; masculine words take **‑em**. "My" and "your" change too:

- **Rozmawiam z twoją siostrą.** — I am talking with your sister.
- **Ona rozmawia z moją babcią.** — She is talking with my grandmother.

In the plural the ending is usually **‑ami**, but these two family words are irregular:

- **My bawimy się z naszymi dziećmi.** — We are playing with our children.
- **Idziemy na spacer z braćmi.** — We are going for a walk with brothers.

## "He is my brother"

Saying who someone is with **jest** also uses the instrumental:

- **On jest moim bratem.** — He is my brother.
- **On jest twoim ojcem** — He is your father

> **Watch out:** **z** becomes **ze** before some hard-to-say groups of consonants: **On żyje z ciotką i ze stryjem.**
