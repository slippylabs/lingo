In this lesson: the verb **to be**, the words for **I, you, he, she**, and **this / that**.

## "To be"

Kashubian changes the verb for each person:

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| I / we | **jem** | **jesmë** |
| you / you all | **jes** | **jesce** |
| he, she, it / they | **je** | **są** |

- **Jô jem mądri.** — I am wise.
- **Chto të jes?** — Who are you?
- **Òna je baro mądrô** — She is very wise
- **Më jesmë baro mądri** — We are very wise
- **To są przëjacelé.** — These are friends.

## "Not"

Put **nie** before the verb:

- **To nie je mòja sostra.** — This is not my sister.

## This and that

**ten**, **ta** and **to** mean "this" (or "that"); the form follows the word's gender, its grammatical group (masculine, feminine or neuter). **tamten** and **tamta** point further away: "that one over there".

- **Ten chłop je miłi.** — That man is kind.
- **Ta białka je dobrô.** — This woman is good.
- **Tamten dom je snôżi.** — That house over there is beautiful.

## Describing words follow the noun's gender

A describing word (an adjective) changes its ending to match:

- **dobri człowiek** — a good person (masculine)
- **Ta białka je dobrô.** — This woman is good. (feminine)
- **To je dobré aùto?** — Is this a good car? (neuter)

> **Watch out:** "they" has two forms. **Òni** is for groups that include men; **Òne** is for all other groups: **Òni są baro miłi.** but **Òne są baro miłé.**
