In this lesson: **hobbies** and how a verb's **ending** shows who is doing it.

## Endings show the person

You can often drop "he" or "we", because the ending already says who:

- he / she: **‑тъ** — **Она пишєтъ письма.** — She is writing letters.
- we: **‑мъ** — **Мꙑ играѥмъ въ шахъ.** — We are playing chess.
- they: **‑ѭтъ** — **Они слꙋшаѭтъ мꙋзикѫ.** — They are listening to music.
- you (one person): **‑ши** — **Тꙑ глѧдѣши сєриѭ?** — Are you watching a series?
- you (more than one): **‑тє** — **Вꙑ оучитє сѧ ѥзꙑка.** — You are learning a language.

## Ongoing actions

Slavic verbs come in pairs: one for an action that is going on or repeated, one for a single finished action. This lesson uses only the first kind:

- **Она сѣдитъ и читаѥтъ.** — She is sitting and reading.
- **Онъ пишєтъ дьньвникъ.** — He is writing a diary.

## "To learn" has a little extra word

**оучити сѧ** is literally "to teach oneself". Keep the **сѧ**:

- **Оучимъ сѧ тѫ.** — We are learning here.

> **Watch out:** you play a game **въ**: **Онъ играѥтъ въ фꙋтболъ**, but an instrument **на**: **Она играѥтъ на гитарꙋ**.
