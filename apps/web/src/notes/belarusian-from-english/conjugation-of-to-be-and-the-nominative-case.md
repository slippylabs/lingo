In this lesson: saying **who or what someone is**, the words for **this and that**, and **masculine, feminine and neuter** words.

## No word for "is"

In the present tense Belarusian simply leaves out "am", "is" and "are":

- **Я мудры.** — I am wise.
- **Ён добры сябар** — He is a good friend
- **Вы добрыя.** — You are good.
- **Хто ты?** — Who are you?

The word cards list forms like **я ёсць**, but in normal sentences you leave the verb out. **ёсць** is mainly used for "there is".

## I, you, he, she…

| | one | more than one |
|---|---|---|
| I / we | **я** | **мы** |
| you | **ты** | **вы** |
| he, she, it / they | **ён**, **яна**, **яно** | **яны** |

## Masculine, feminine, neuter

Every noun belongs to one of three groups called genders. The ending usually tells you which:

- masculine: ends in a consonant, like **дом** and **горад**
- feminine: ends in ‑а or ‑я, like **жанчына** and **сястра**
- neuter: often ends in ‑о or ‑е; **дзіця** (child) is neuter too

Describing words take a matching ending:

- **Той дзень халодны.** — That day is cold. (masculine ‑ы)
- **Тая жанчына высокая.** — That woman is tall. (feminine ‑ая)
- **Гэта добрае аўта?** — Is this a good car? (neuter ‑ае)
- **Гэта добрыя сябры?** — Are these good friends? (plural ‑ыя)

"My" matches as well: **мой дом**, **мая сястра**, **маё дзіця**.

## This and that

- **гэты** / **гэтая** — this (masculine / feminine)
- **той** / **тая** — that
- **вунь той** — that over there

On its own, **гэта** means "this is" or "these are":

- **Гэта мая сястра.** — This is my sister.
- **Гэта не твой дом.** — This is not your house.

> **Watch out:** **ты** is for one person you know well; **вы** is for several people, or to be polite to one.
