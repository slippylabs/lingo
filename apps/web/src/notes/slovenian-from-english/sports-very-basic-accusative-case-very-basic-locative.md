In this lesson: **sports**, the form for **what you play**, and **where** something happens.

## What you play: the accusative case

The thing that receives the action (the object) takes the *accusative case*. Feminine words ending in **‑a** change it to **‑o**:

- **košarka** — basketball
- **Ona igra košarko.** — She plays basketball.

Masculine words for things stay the same:

- **On igra tenis.** — He plays tennis.
- **Igram nogomet.** — I play football.

## Not playing: the genitive case

After "not", the object takes another form, the *genitive case*. Masculine words add **‑a**:

- **Jaz ne igram hokeja.** — I do not play hockey.

## Where: the locative case

After **v** (in) and **na** (on, at), when saying where something is, words take the *locative case*. Many add **‑u**, feminine words in **‑a** change it to **‑i**:

- **Stadion je v mestu.** — The stadium is in the city.
- **Ona igra na stadionu.** — She plays at the stadium.
- **Mi plavamo v reki.** — We swim in the river.

## More sport phrases

- **On trenira vsak dan.** — He trains every day.
- **Oni tečejo zjutraj.** — They run in the morning.
- **Plavanje je dobro za zdravje.** — Swimming is good for health.

> **Watch out:** the word for the game changes after "not": **igram nogomet** but **ne igram hokeja**.
