In this lesson: **drinks** and the **genitive case**, the form after **bez** (without), **z** (from) and in "a bottle of".

## The genitive

The genitive case is the "of" form. It follows **bez**, **z** and amounts like a bottle or a glass:

- **Piješ bez mléka?** — Do you drink without milk? (**mléko** → **mléka**)
- **Já miluji džus bez cukru.** — I love juice without sugar.
- **Ty máš láhev džusu?** — Do you have a bottle of juice?
- **Láhev vody leží na stole.** — A bottle of water is lying on the table. (**voda** → **vody**)
- **Sklenici vína pro mě, prosím.** — A glass of wine for me, please.
- **To víno je z Polska.** — This wine is from Poland.

So neuter ‑o becomes ‑a, feminine ‑a becomes ‑y, and many masculine nouns add ‑u.

## Not having or not drinking

After a "not" verb, modern Czech keeps the normal accusative form:

- **Já nemám vodu.** — I do not have water.
- **On nemá kávu.** — He does not have coffee.
- **Ona nepije čaj.** — She does not drink tea.

## Please

- **Dejte mi sklenici vína, prosím.** — Give me a glass of wine, please.

> **Watch out:** "a bottle of" and "a glass of" need the genitive: **láhev vody**, **sklenici vína**, never *láhev voda*.
