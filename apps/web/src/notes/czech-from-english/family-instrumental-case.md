In this lesson: **family words** and the **instrumental case**, the form a noun takes after **s** ("with").

## "With" changes the noun

A case is a form a noun takes for its job in the sentence. After **s** (with), Czech uses the instrumental case:

| | basic form | with |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | **syn** | **se synem** |
| feminine | **matka** | **s matkou** |
| feminine | **rodina** | **s rodinou** |

So masculine nouns add ‑em and feminine ‑a becomes ‑ou:

- **On mluví s dědem.** — He is talking with his grandfather.
- **Ona jde s matkou.** — She is going with her mother.
- **Oni jedou s tetou.** — They are going with their aunt.
- **Jdu do parku s rodinou.** — I am going to the park with the family.

## More than one person

- **Jdeme na procházku s bratry.** — We are going for a walk with brothers.
- **My hrajeme s našimi dětmi.** — We are playing with our children.

## "My" and "your" change too

- **Mluvím s tvojí sestrou.** — I am talking with your sister.
- **Ona mluví s mojí babičkou.** — She is talking with my grandmother.
- **S kým ty jdeš?** — Who are you going with?

**s** becomes **se** before a word starting with s or z: **On žije s tetou a se strýcem.** — He lives with his aunt and uncle.

> **Watch out:** **On je mým bratrem** (He is my brother) uses the same instrumental form after **je**. That is careful, formal Czech; in everyday speech people say *On je můj bratr*.
