In this lesson: the verb **to be**, the words for **I, you, he, she**, and **this / that**.

## "To be"

Czech changes the verb for each person:

| who | "to be" |
|---|---|
| I | **jsem** |
| you (one friend) | **jsi** |
| he / she / it | **je** |
| we | **jsme** |
| you (several people, or polite) | **jste** |
| they | **jsou** |

- **Já jsem moudrý.** — I am wise.
- **My jsme velmi moudří** — We are very wise
- **Vy jste dobří.** — You are good.
- **To jsou přátelé.** — These are friends.

The ending already shows who you mean, so **já**, **ty**, **vy** and the rest are often left out:

- **Jste také moudří?** — Are you also wise?

## Masculine, feminine, neuter

Every Czech noun belongs to a gender, a grammar group. The last letter usually tells you which:

- masculine, often a consonant: **dům** — house
- feminine, often ‑a: **žena** — woman
- neuter, often ‑o or ‑e: **město** — city

A describing word changes its ending to match:

- **To je dobrý člověk.** — This is a good person.
- **Ta žena je dobrá.** — This woman is good.
- **To je příjemné město.** — This is a pleasant city.

## This and that

- **Ten muž je milý.** — That man is kind. (**ten** for masculine)
- **Ta žena je vysoká.** — That woman is tall. (**ta** for feminine)
- **Tamten dům je krásný.** — That house over there is beautiful.
- **To je moje sestra.** — This is my sister. (**to je** = this is)

## Saying "not"

Put *ne‑* in front of the verb. **je** becomes **není**:

- **To není tvůj dům.** — This is not your house.

> **Watch out:** "they" has two forms. **oni** is for a group with at least one man, **ony** for women only, and the describing word follows: **Oni jsou velmi milí** but **Ony jsou velmi milé**.
