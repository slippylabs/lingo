In this lesson: **food** and the **accusative case**, the form of the thing you eat, buy or cook.

## The accusative

The accusative case is the form of the thing that receives the action. Only some nouns change:

- feminine ‑a becomes ‑u: **My vidíme tvou pizzu na stole.** — We see your pizza on the table.
- feminine ‑e becomes ‑i: **My jíme večeři.** — We are eating dinner.
- masculine things and neuter nouns stay the same: **Já jím chléb.** — I am eating bread.
- **Ona jí maso.** — She is eating meat.
- **Oni mají snídani.** — They are having breakfast.

## "To eat" is irregular

- **Já jím chléb.** — I am eating bread.
- **Ty jíš vejce?** — Are you eating an egg?
- **On pije mléko a jí maso.** — He is drinking milk and eating meat.
- **My jíme večeři.** — We are eating dinner.

## Liking something

*mít rád* (literally "to have gladly") means "to like". A man says **rád**, a woman **ráda**:

- **On má rád mléko.** — He likes milk.
- **Ona ráda vaří večeři.** — She likes to cook dinner.
- **Já vím, že ty máš rád vejce.** — I know that you like eggs.

> **Watch out:** **Já koupím máslo** means "I will buy butter". **koupit** is a one-time, finished action, so its present form talks about the future. For "they are buying", the course uses **Oni kupují máslo a chléb**.
