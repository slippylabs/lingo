In this lesson: a **review of the cases**: the endings that show a noun's job in the sentence.

## The cases you've met

- *nominative*, the dictionary form, for the subject: **Вона є в місті.** — She is in the city.
- *accusative*, the thing an action is done to: **Він бачить свою жінку.** — He sees his wife.
- *genitive*, for "of" and "there is no": **Брата тут нема.** — There is no brother here.
- *dative*, for "to someone": **Дам жінці.** — I will give to the woman.
- *instrumental*, for "with" and tools: **Говоримо з друзями.** — We talk with friends.
- *locative*, after **в** and **на** for "where": **Вона є в місті.** — She is in the city.

## "There is no …"

**нема** ("there is no") always takes the genitive:

- **Кості тут нема.** — There is no bone here.
- **Вже нема бананів у домі.** — There are no more bananas in the house.
- **Імені він не має.** — He has no name.

## A tool without "with"

- **Це можна різати ножем.** — This can be cut with a knife.

## Plural endings

- **Вона дає дар братам.** — She gives a gift to the brothers. (dative)
- **Вона говорить про братів.** — She talks about brothers. (about + accusative)

> **Watch out:** "there is no" is never followed by the plain form: **Брата тут нема**, from **брат**.
