In this lesson: **family words** and saying **"with someone"**.

## "With" changes the ending

After **з** ("with") a noun takes the instrumental case: a special form used for "with someone" and "by means of something".

| | dictionary form | with … |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | **дід** | **з дідом** |
| masculine | **син** | **з сином** |
| feminine | **тітка** | **з тіткою** |
| feminine | **дочка** | **з дочкою** |

- **Він говорить з дідом.** — He is talking with his grandfather.
- **Він живе з сином.** — He lives with his son.
- **Вони їдуть з тіткою.** — They are going with their aunt.
- **Вона йде з мамою.** — She is going with her mother.

Masculine words take **‑ом**; feminine words ending in ‑а take **‑ою**.

## More than one, and "my/your"

Plural nouns take **‑ами** or **‑ми**, and "my", "your", "our" change too:

- **Йдемо на прогулянку з братами.** — We are going for a walk with brothers.
- **Ми граємо з нашими дітьми.** — We are playing with our children.
- **Говорю з твоєю сестрою.** — I am talking with your sister.
- **Вона говорить з моєю бабусею.** — She is talking with my grandmother.

## Asking "with whom?"

- **З ким ти йдеш?** — Who are you going with?

> **Watch out:** without **з** the word stays plain: **Він мій брат.** — He is my brother. Only "with" brings in the new ending.
