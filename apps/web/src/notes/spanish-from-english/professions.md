## Jobs have a masculine and a feminine form

Most job words change their ending for a man or a woman:

| man | woman |
|---|---|
| **médico** | **médica** |
| **bombero** | **bombera** |
| **granjero** | **granjera** |

- **La médica bebe agua** — The (female) doctor drinks water
- **El granjero ve los animales** — The farmer sees the animals
- **Las bomberas llegan** — The firefighters arrive

Some words ending in **-e** stay the same and only the article changes: **el cantante** / *la cantante* (singer).

## More -ar verbs

Verbs ending in **-ar** take **-a** for he/she and **-an** for they:

- **El camarero trabaja** — The waiter works
- **El cantante canta bien** — The singer sings well
- **Las bomberas llegan** — The firefighters arrive

**Common mistake:** after **ser** you don't need "a" for a job: **Ella es enfermera** — She is a nurse (no *una*).
