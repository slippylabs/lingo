# Czech: unsure / not fixed

## Template-wide (likely all 16 Slavic courses)
- EVERY mini-dictionary gloss is the literal string "undefined" (4,347/4,347 in Czech; Polish, Russian and
  Interslavic checked: same). The exported challenges carry 7,707 `"definition": "undefined"` hints, so learners
  see "undefined" when they tap a word. Not fixable per entry here; needs an app-side fix (hide undefined hints)
  or generated glosses. New entries added by my fixes carry real Czech glosses where I had one.
- The course content is machine-made from a Polish/Interslavic template: genitive-after-negation, 'li' questions,
  'výše' comparatives, ordinal time-telling and Interslavic grammar terms (Místník) leak into each language.
  Sibling courses likely have the same pattern.

## Left alone (plausible, not certain enough)
- ten = that / to, ta = this: ten/ta/to mean both this and that; the course is inconsistent but not wrong.
- děd (grandfather) is bookish; everyday Czech is děda/dědeček.
- "Jsi ty bez láhve?", "Čteš ty romány?", "Jsi ty Angličanem?": emphatic 'ty' is unusual but grammatical.
- "Tamto ráno je chladné" (that morning over there) is odd but grammatical.
- "Mne nevidíš" (literary mne vs everyday mě).
- "Hledíme k severu", "Oči hledí do knihy": hledět is bookish for 'look', not wrong.
- "linie" (line) where everyday Czech says čára; "oblaky" vs mraky; "temný" vs tmavý.
- "Na pánvi se vaří má palačinka" (pancakes are fried, smažit) - kept.
- "Jmenuji se Bartek" uses a Polish name.
- numbers10100.md "41 psů (governed by 'jeden')": Czech says čtyřicet jeden pes or jednačtyřicet psů; unsure which to teach.
- Word "spodní prádlo/kalhoty" (spodní kalhoty = long johns), "zdravý/ahoj = healthy/hello", duplicate
  "pracovat (fyzicky)/(obecně)" both "to work" - template artefacts, left.
